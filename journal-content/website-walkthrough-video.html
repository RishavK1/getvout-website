A website walkthrough should answer a practical question: what can a visitor do here, and what happens next? A useful recording demonstrates a task. It does not need to prove that every section can move.

What should the walkthrough show?

The short preview above is an actual browser capture of JAC Creation, one of the projects in our collection. It is a silent, eight-second scroll preview, not a narrated tutorial, a client testimonial or evidence of commercial results. Open the live project to inspect the experience directly.

For an explanatory video, choose one journey before recording: explore a service, compare two products or find a contact route. Start at a recognizable point and stop after the useful outcome. If the topic needs several unrelated journeys, separate them rather than rushing through each.

How do you plan a product demo?

Write a short outline with three parts: the person’s question, the action they take and the result they should expect. Capture the real interface with synthetic data wherever personal information could appear. Keep invented example names clearly separate from real customers and results.

Show pauses and transitions that help someone follow the action. A recording edited into an impressive montage may remove the very context a new visitor needs. For support material, understandable sequencing is more important than keeping every cut on a beat.

What about accessibility and alternatives?

A silent scrolling preview does not explain every visual detail. The text on this page identifies the project and purpose; the linked live site gives a different way to explore it. For a full instructional recording, plan an equivalent text explanation and any captions, audio description or other alternatives required by its content and audience.

W3C’s media guidance is useful because “add captions” is not the entire job. Spoken information, meaningful sounds and information that exists only in the picture need different consideration. Decide what the recording communicates before choosing the alternative.

Should the video load immediately?

Not necessarily. The existing player waits for an intentional play action. This keeps the first view focused and avoids loading this clip before someone asks for it. If you use a third-party host, explain that relationship and review its privacy and performance implications before embedding it.

For a business site, connect the recording to a useful next step: inspect the project, read the implementation notes or ask a question. A follower count is not a substitute for evidence that the walkthrough helped someone complete a task.
