A responsive interaction gives useful visual feedback promptly. That is different from finishing every animation quickly. A menu can acknowledge input immediately and then unfold; it can also have a short animation that starts too late because the main thread is busy.

What does INP actually measure?

Interaction to Next Paint, or INP, assesses responsiveness to clicks, taps and keyboard interactions across a visit. It looks at the delay until the browser can present the next frame, rather than waiting for every asynchronous consequence of the action to finish.

web.dev defines a good INP as 200 milliseconds or less at the 75th percentile of field page loads, segmented by mobile and desktop. That is a metric threshold, not an instruction that every hover or page transition should last 200 milliseconds. Ordinary scrolling and hovering are not themselves the interactions INP measures.

Why can a short animation still feel slow?

Suppose a visitor opens a navigation menu while a large script is processing. If the browser cannot handle the input promptly, even a brief visual transition begins after an uncomfortable pause. Reducing its duration does not address the blocked input.

Investigate what happens before the next paint: expensive event handlers, competing work and unnecessary rendering are possible contributors. Give understandable feedback while longer work continues. Do not display a success state merely because an animation ended.

Where should easing fit?

Easing controls how movement changes over time. It can help communicate an element’s destination or relationship to the page, but there is no universal curve that makes a website feel premium. Choose motion around the task, then test interruption, repeated activation and slower devices.

How should reduced motion work?

The prefers-reduced-motion media query communicates a user preference for less motion. Keep the result and meaning of the interaction while reducing non-essential movement. Removing a decorative translation may be appropriate; hiding the only indication that something changed is not.

The existing demonstrations keep their motion preferences. For your own build, test the reduced-motion path as a real interface, not as a screenshot. Check whether controls remain reachable, content appears and the selected or completed state is understandable.

What can we measure before launch?

Exercise the important journeys in development tools and look for delayed feedback. Laboratory tests can reproduce problems but do not establish your real visitors’ 75th-percentile INP. After launch, use suitable field data when available and investigate the particular interactions behind slow results.
