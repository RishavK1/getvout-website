A useful call to action tells people what will happen, can be reached and operated, and gives understandable feedback. Animation can support that experience, but it cannot repair an unclear promise or a destination that does not match the label.

What should a CTA button say?

Start with the next real step. “Request a consultation” and “Buy now” describe different commitments. “Get started” may be appropriate when nearby content explains the next screen; in isolation it leaves more work to the reader. Review the label together with the heading, supporting text and destination rather than choosing a persuasive phrase first.

A service website also needs to distinguish an enquiry from a purchase. If a project requires a conversation before a quote, say so. Do not imply instant booking, a fixed price or immediate delivery when the workflow cannot provide it.

Should it be a link or a button?

Use a link when the action navigates to a destination. Use a button for an action in the current interface, such as opening a dialog or submitting a form. Their appearance can be similar; their semantics and expected keyboard behavior are not interchangeable. A clickable container is not an automatic replacement for either.

How large should the target be?

WCAG 2.2’s level-AA minimum target-size criterion uses 24 by 24 CSS pixels, subject to exceptions including spacing and inline links. The enhanced criterion uses 44 by 44. A larger, comfortably spaced primary action is often a sensible design choice, but “44 pixels is the universal minimum” is not an accurate summary of the standard.

Measure the interactive area, not just the visible arrow. Check nearby controls, narrow screens and longer translated labels. A generous visual button can still be difficult to use if a transparent layer intercepts its input.

Where does motion help?

The live example above lets you inspect hover, press, focus and pointer-following feedback. These effects demonstrate a visual treatment, not a measured conversion uplift. The action must remain understandable without hover and usable when reduced motion is requested.

Separate immediate acknowledgement from eventual completion. A pressed state can show that an input was received; it must not claim that a message arrived before the system knows that. For a slow operation, communicate progress and prevent unintended duplicate actions where necessary.

A CTA review before launch

  • Follow the destination and compare it with the wording.
  • Tab to the action and check that focus is visible.
  • Operate it with the expected keyboard controls.
  • Test on touch without relying on hover cues.
  • Enable reduced motion and repeat the task.
  • Check failure and retry states, not just the successful animation.

Will a better button increase conversions?

Not by a predictable amount. Evaluate the complete journey, including qualified enquiries and task completion, rather than treating clicks as proof of business value. A clearer label may produce fewer but more appropriate clicks. That can be a better outcome than making an unclear action more attractive.
